A linked pair always matches itself. But touching an outside tile can reveal both partners before you bring them together. And a new pair takes two spaces. Plan around the whole board.

What is quantum here?

Python uses Qiskit to prepare and measure one-qubit states and Bell pairs. The browser asks the same engine for exact probabilities. Linked tiles use (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2, with 0 = 2 and 1 = 4.

These matching statistics can also be reproduced with classical shared randomness. Correlation alone does not prove entanglement or a quantum advantage. The notebook separately demonstrates coherence and interference. All results here use a local simulator.

Tiles resolve only at a possible merge contact, even when the attempted merge fails. A merge creates one fixed tile. A tile merges at most once per swipe. Every fourth active turn can spawn a pair, every second a single uncertain tile, within a four-qubit cap. Other spawns are ordinary 2s or 4s.
